# Pokhara Job Market — Pilot Findings

**Source:** Adarsha Samaj e-paper, 10 issues (2013–2026)
**Sample:** 93 distinct job advertisements, 196 role-level records
**Date:** 2026-09-30

---

## Read this first

**This is not a market survey. It is 93 advertisements, and 68% of them are from 2013–2016.**

The 10 issues were selected to test whether the extraction pipeline could read different decades of typesetting — 2012–2019 uses different fonts, page sizes and layouts than 2020+. They were **not** chosen at random, and they are **not** proportional to the archive.

Only **13 ads (14%) are from 2026.** The current Pokhara labour market is the thing we most want to describe and the thing this sample describes least.

So: read every percentage below as *what this sample shows*, never as *market share*. What this document is genuinely good for is telling us **which questions the full archive can answer**, and giving early signal on where the interesting patterns are.

A representative picture needs the full run. See §8.

---

## 1. What employers are asking for

93 distinct ads, each assigned to one sector from its role titles and employer name. (Multi-role ads are counted once, under the sector most of their roles fall in.)

| Sector | Ads | Share of sample |
|---|---|---|
| Admin / office & general staff | 24 | 26% |
| Teaching & training | 22 | 24% |
| Other / unclassified | 21 | 23% |
| Hotel, restaurant & housekeeping | 18 | 19% |
| Security / guarding | 6 | 6% |
| Transport (drivers) | 1 | 1% |
| Cleaning / labour | 1 | 1% |

**The single clearest pattern: this is a service-sector labour market.** Admin, teaching, hospitality and security together account for 75% of ads. There is almost no evidence of manufacturing or trades hiring in this sample beyond a single furniture-industry helper ad and a plumbing ad.

"Other" at 23% is largely generic Nepali listings — `कर्मचारी` (staff), `साधारण कर्मचारी` (general staff), `+2 उत्तीर्ण कर्मचारी` — which are real jobs but say nothing about the role.

## 2. The market shifted between 2013 and now

Sector mix by year of publication (one sector per ad):

| Year | Ads | Dominant sectors |
|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 31 | Other 13, Admin 7, Security 4, Hotel/Food 4 |
| 2016 | 32 | Teaching 12, Admin 8, Hotel/Food 7, Other 5 |
| 2019 | 11 | Admin 5, Security 2, Teaching 2, Other 2 |
| 2022 | 6 | Teaching 3, Admin 3 |
| 2026 | 13 | **Hotel/Food 7**, Teaching 3, Other 1, Admin 1 |

Two movements stand out:

**Hospitality is the most prominent 2026 sector** — 7 of 13 current ads (54%). It was 4 of 31 (13%) in 2013 and 7 of 32 (22%) in 2016, and **zero in both 2019 and 2022**. A jump from absent to over half the sample is the largest movement in the data. It is either a genuine growth story or an artefact of which modern issues we happened to pick — the full archive would settle it quickly.

**Foreign employment collapsed after 2013.** In 2013, 11 of 31 ads (35%) were foreign-employment or manpower ads. In 2016 there were **none**. Only one appears from 2019 onward.

| Era | Foreign ads | Share of era |
|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 11 | 35% |
| 2016 | 0 | 0% |
| 2019 | 1 | 9% |
| 2022 | 0 | 0% |
| 2026 | 0 | 0% |

This is consistent with Nepal's 2017 ban on citizen labour departures to the Gulf, which removed most of that advertising. **Treat this as a hypothesis worth confirming across the full archive** — but it is a strong, plausible signal, and it materially changes what the historical dataset can be used for.

## 4. Salaries: mostly disclosed, mostly not

Only **93 of 196 role records (47%) state any salary at all**. Of those, many decline to give a figure:

- `आपसी छलफलबाट तय गरिनेछ` — "to be decided by mutual discussion"
- `संस्थाको नियमअनुसार` — "as per organisation rules"
- `नेपाल सरकारको प्रचलित नियमानुसार` — "as per prevailing government rules"
- `आकर्षक` — "attractive" (with vehicle fare and bonus)

Where figures *are* printed, they cluster tightly by role:

**2016 — private school teaching (Bhasker Memorial School, one ad, 7 posts)**

| Role | Monthly |
|---|---|
| Vice-Principal | Rs 40,000 |
| Teacher (Science), Secondary | Rs 25,400–35,000 |
| Teacher (Maths), Lower Secondary | Rs 19,200 |
| Teacher (English), Primary | Rs 18,730 |
| Teacher (Pre-Primary, Montessori trained) | Rs 15,000 |

This is the most transparent employer in the sample — a full published salary band per post, and the ad also publishes its written-exam and interview dates. **Employers who publish bands are rare and worth flagging as a category of their own.**

**2013 — general and security staff**

| Role | Monthly |
|---|---|
| Security Guard | Rs 70,000 / Rs 49,000 / Rs 45,225 |
| Plumber | Rs 26,400 |
| General Staff | Rs 4,500 |
| Marketing Officer | Rs 10,000 |
| Management / Front Office Trainee | Rs 8,500 |
| Staff (SLC passed) | Rs 15,500 |

Note the **Rs 4,500 to Rs 70,000 spread within 2013 alone** — roughly 15×. Security guarding pays far above general staff, which is the most striking wage differential in the data.

**Caution:** these are single-ad snapshots from five years apart. Inflation between 2013 and 2026 in Nepal is substantial, so **the 2013 and 2026 figures are not comparable as printed.** The full archive would let us compute real-terms trends; this sample cannot.

## 5. Requirements: experience over credentials

**Only 40 of 196 records (20%) state any education requirement.** Most hiring here is experience-based, not credential-gated.

Where requirements are stated, they cluster at **+2 (Higher Secondary) as the ceiling**, with **SLC (Secondary)** common as the floor. Bachelor-level requirements appear only in the better-paid professional and training roles.

**Contact is overwhelmingly mobile.** 142 of 196 records (72%) cite a mobile number. The application channel in this market is a phone call, not a form, not an email.

## 6. Employers advertising repeatedly

| Employer | Ads |
|---|---|
| Hotel Silent Peak | 5 |
| Fuji Japanese Language Centre | 2 |
| Anjali Overseas Service Pvt. Ltd. | 2 |
| The River Overseas Pvt. Ltd. | 2 |

Hotel Silent Peak advertised 5 times — once as a single "Night Staff" ad, once as a multi-role hotel staff ad, and in consecutive issues. **Repeat advertising is a decent proxy for ongoing recruitment**, and it also means naive counting inflates any employer-level total.

Two of the four repeat advertisers are **overseas-job agencies** (Anjali Overseas, The River Overseas) — consistent with the foreign-employment pattern in §2.

## 8. What the full archive would let us answer

Each of these is a question this sample can only hint at, and the full run can settle:

1. **Has foreign-employment advertising actually collapsed**, and in which year? Our 2013→2016 cliff is based on 2 issues.
2. **Is hospitality genuinely growing**, or is the 2026 signal an artefact of sampling 5 of 10 modern issues?
3. **What are real-terms salary trends** across 14 years, net of inflation?
4. **Which employers recruit repeatedly**, and in which sectors — the best source of a steady pipeline of live jobs for the page?
5. **How do government vacancy cycles run** — are they seasonal, tied to fiscal years?
6. **Which roles are oversupplied** (generic `कर्मचारी`) vs genuinely scarce?
7. **2018 is missing from the archive entirely** (89 of 356 issues survived). Any trend crossing 2018 has a hole in it.

---

## 9. Honest limitations

- **93 ads is not a market.** Treat percentages as sample descriptions, never market share.
- **68% of the sample is 2013–2016.** The current market is represented by 13 ads.
- **Issues were chosen for typesetting diversity, not randomly.** Sector and employer shares are therefore not representative by design.
- **13 of 218 extracted records (6.0%) were course catalogues and study-abroad ads, not jobs.** They have been excluded from every figure above. The remaining `other` category (3 ads) is unreviewed.
- **Salaries across eras are not comparable as printed** without inflation adjustment.
- **Repeat advertisers mean counts overstate distinct openings** unless deduplicated by date window.
- **One issue per year for 2013–2022.** A single issue cannot represent a year.

---

## 10. Bottom line

The useful signal here is directional, not statistical:

- Pokhara's classified hiring is **overwhelmingly service-sector** — admin, hospitality, teaching, security.
- **Hospitality is the strongest current signal** (7 of 13 in 2026, versus zero in 2019 and 2022).
- **Foreign-employment advertising appears to have collapsed** after 2013, plausibly tied to Nepal's 2017 Gulf departure ban.
- **Pay is extremely uneven** — Rs 4,500 to Rs 70,000 within a single year.
- **Hiring is mobile-first and experience-led**, with +2 as the usual ceiling.

None of these should be published or acted on as fact. All of them are testable in a day of extraction once disk and the recall gate are resolved — and §8 lists exactly what to check first.
